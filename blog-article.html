The long-term residency routes into Thailand, compared

Retirement, marriage, Thailand Privilege, the LTR and the business route all end in a visa — but they differ in cost, paperwork and how easily they survive a change in your circumstances. A side-by-side reading.

Almost every enquiry we receive starts with the same sentence: “I want to live in Thailand — which visa do I need?” It is the wrong first question. The right one is how long you intend to stay, what you will be doing, and how much annual friction you are willing to absorb.

Five routes account for the overwhelming majority of long-stay permissions granted to foreign nationals in Thailand. Each is a different trade between money, documentation and flexibility. Below we set them beside one another as we would in a first consultation — plainly, and with the failure modes named.

Before you read on

This article is general information about how these routes work in practice. It is not legal advice, and immigration policy in Thailand is revised regularly. Confirm the current requirements — and how they apply to your own nationality, age and finances — before acting on anything here.

Why the choice matters more than the visa

A visa is simply the permission you hold on the day you enter. What governs your life afterwards is the extension of stay — the annual renewal granted inside Thailand, the reporting that comes with it, and the conditions that can quietly invalidate it. Two people can hold identical stamps and have completely different experiences, because one chose a route that fits their circumstances and the other did not.

The most common and most expensive mistake we see is choosing the route with the lowest headline cost, then spending years managing paperwork it was never designed to carry — a business structure maintained purely to justify a visa, for instance, with the tax and audit obligations that come attached.

Choose the route that survives your worst year, not the one that suits your best one.

The retirement route

For applicants over the qualifying age, the retirement extension remains the most straightforward long-stay option. It rests on demonstrating a financial position — held either as a deposit in a Thai bank account seasoned for a required period, as a monthly income, or as a combination of the two.

Its strengths are simplicity and independence: it does not depend on an employer, a spouse or a company. Its weaknesses are the annual renewal cycle, the 90-day address reporting, and the fact that funds committed to the bank requirement are not freely available for the periods immigration expects them to remain in place.

  • Best for applicants who meet the age threshold and can ring-fence funds.
  • Watch for: the seasoning window before and after the extension date.
  • Does not, by itself, permit you to work in Thailand.

Marriage and family routes

An extension based on marriage to a Thai national — or on supporting a Thai child — carries a lower financial threshold than retirement, which is why it is attractive. In exchange it is more document-heavy and more personal: expect home visits, photographs, witness statements and a level of scrutiny that surprises applicants who have only dealt with tourist entries before.

It is also the route most exposed to changes in personal circumstances. A separation does not merely end a relationship; it ends the legal basis of your stay, usually with very little notice.

Thailand Privilege membership

The membership programme sells convenience. You pay a substantial one-off fee and receive multi-year permission to stay with a materially reduced administrative burden — no annual financial evidence, and concierge handling of the routine reporting.

For clients who value their time above the fee, and whose income sits offshore, it is often the rational choice. It is not a work permit, it does not lead to permanent residency, and the tiers and pricing have been restructured more than once — so read the current terms rather than an article about them.

The Long-Term Resident visa

The LTR was introduced for a narrower audience: wealthy individuals, retirees with substantial pensions, remote professionals employed by established foreign companies, and highly skilled specialists. Where an applicant fits one of those categories cleanly, it is the strongest package available — a long permission, relaxed reporting, and a digital work permit for the qualifying categories.

The difficulty is documentary. Qualification turns on evidence of income, assets, employment history and, in some categories, the size and revenue of the employer abroad. Applications fail on proof, not on eligibility.

The business route

A non-immigrant B visa supported by a Thai company, followed by a work permit and an annual extension, is the route for people who intend to earn income in Thailand. It is the only option on this list that legitimately combines residence with the right to work.

It is also the most demanding. The company must be genuinely capitalised, employ Thai staff in the required ratio, file monthly and annual returns, and survive an audit. Maintaining a company solely to hold a visa is both expensive and, where the structure is artificial, legally fragile.

  1. Incorporate and capitalise the company properly.
  2. Register for tax and social security; begin filing from month one.
  3. Obtain the non-immigrant B visa, then the work permit.
  4. Extend the stay annually, on the strength of the company's filings.

Comparing cost against friction

Set the five routes side by side and a pattern appears. Cost and friction sit at opposite ends of a see-saw: the membership programme and the LTR buy administrative calm at a high entry price, while retirement, marriage and the business route are cheaper to begin and heavier to carry.

The right answer depends on which currency you are shorter of. Clients with time and patience usually choose retirement or marriage. Clients whose weeks are already full almost always regret not paying for the quieter route.

What we usually recommend

If you qualify for the LTR, apply for it — the documentary effort is front-loaded and then largely over. If you meet the retirement threshold and do not mind an annual rhythm, the retirement extension is dependable and inexpensive. Take the business route when you genuinely intend to trade in Thailand, and not as a device to remain here.

Whichever you choose, decide before you enter the country. Switching routes from inside Thailand is possible but rarely convenient, and a wrong first entry can cost you months.
